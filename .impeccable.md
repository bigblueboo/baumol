# Design context — One Hour Is Still One Hour

_Synthesized from DESIGN.md and the build brief (2026-08-11). Edit freely; the
design skills read this file._

## Target audience

Curious general readers — the crowd that follows links to pudding.cool or
Nicky Case explorables. Also students meeting Baumol for the first time and
policy-curious people who suspect "everything getting expensive" has a
villain. They are not economists. Half arrive on a phone. They give it
thirty seconds before deciding whether to stay.

## Use cases

1. Get the Baumol intuition in about two minutes by pressing one big button
   and watching the chain of consequences play out.
2. Poke the levers — substitution, retraining, caps, subsidies,
   distribution — to stress-test "but couldn't we just…?" objections.
3. Leave with one quotable line and a screenshot-worthy readout.

## Brand personality / tone

A warm interactive essay, not a dashboard. Storybook-ledger: a small town
drawn in ink on warm paper, with economics running underneath. Plainspoken,
confident, a little wry. The subject is human time — babysitting! — so the
piece should feel humane, never corporate. Precision shows up in the
numbers and the typography, not in chrome.

## Aesthetic direction

- Warm paper ground, ink text. No dark-mode neon, no glassmorphism.
- Two sector hue families carried everywhere (scene, charts, metrics):
  software/studio = cool indigo-teal; babysitting/care = warm coral-amber.
- Display face: Fraunces (variable, real character, storybook-editorial).
  Body/UI: Instrument Sans. Tabular numerals for every readout.
- One unmissable interaction: the big invention button. One unmissable
  number: what an hour of babysitting costs.
- The clock is the leitmotif. The care-house clock always ticks at the same
  speed; the studio accelerates around it.
